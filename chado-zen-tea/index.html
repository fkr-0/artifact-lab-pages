Four principles

Sen no Rikyū’s formulation is often summarized as wa, kei, sei, and jaku: harmony, respect, purity, and tranquility. Tap a card to use it as the lens for the guide.

Wa

Harmony between host and guest, utensils and season, garden and room. Nothing is merely decoration; each relation is tuned.

Kei

Respect appears in bows, careful handling, and the way people and objects are treated as worthy of attention.

Sei

Purity is cleanliness, simplification, and inward clearing. The act of cleaning utensils is also training the mind.

Jaku

Tranquility is not forced calm; it is the stillness that becomes possible through harmony, respect, and purity.

    Important nuance

    “Zen tea ceremony” is a useful casual phrase, but the Japanese tea ceremony is more precisely called chanoyu, sadō, or chadō. It has roots and affinities with Zen Buddhism, but it is also a cultural art with multiple schools, histories, utensils, architecture, etiquette, seasonal aesthetics, and social contexts.
