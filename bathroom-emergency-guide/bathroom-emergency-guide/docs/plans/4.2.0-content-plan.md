# Bathroom Emergency Guide 4.2.0 — Content Plan

Version 4.2.0 should not become “4.1.2 plus another drawer of facts.” It has two
separate jobs:

1. **Improve the routes already present.**
2. **Extend the guide where the main graph still has real coverage gaps.**

The tracks may be reviewed independently. Improvement work must not be held
hostage by a large new module, and an extension must not smuggle in a rewrite of
everything around it.

## Implementation status — 4.2.0 candidate

Implemented in the current candidate:

- **A1 core:** pass-1 overrides and the four-part action queue are explicit in
  the route catalog and main guide;
- **A3 core:** generated diagrams now read from a machine-readable route map;
- **A4 foundation:** operational source IDs and review dates are structured and
  validated separately from explanatory evidence;
- **B1 complete:** Situation H covers fire, smoke, CO, gas, chemicals,
  electricity, official outdoor warnings, handoff, and aftercare;
- **B2 core complete:** nine dependency/access modifiers plus a full essential
  medication and powered-device continuity route;
- **format parity:** all additions are required in A4 and A4/2, color and mono.

Deliberately carried forward:

- **A2:** full editorial lexicon and repository-wide terminology lint;
- **A5:** broader non-visual, non-breath-focused, large-print, and communication
  access variants;
- **B3:** the complete four-way “no safe place” split with localized service
  destinations;
- **B4:** a separate evacuate-versus-shelter worksheet for flood, heat, smoke,
  and official area warnings.

This is not a paper implementation report. The implemented packets are present
in chapters, data, diagrams, validators, and the canonical build.

## Track A — Improvement of existing content

### A1. One-minute route compression

Audit every chapter opening and every red-flag section. The first visible block
must answer four questions in order:

$$Q=[\text{action},\text{backup},\text{escalation},\text{destination}]$$

Work:

- remove repeated preambles before the first action;
- use direct, polite imperatives;
- keep one sentence per action where possible;
- move mechanisms, statistics, and philosophy below “understand later” headings;
- make uncertainty itself an explicit escalation condition.

Definition of done:

- every A–H route and every destination chapter exposes the four-part queue;
- no joke, source note, formula, or classification sits between a red flag and
  its action;
- text-only and diagram routes produce the same destination.

### A2. Terminology and tone consistency

Create a small editorial lexicon covering:

- `call 112` versus `contact 116 117`;
- `life danger`, `lasting harm`, `urgent`, and `non-life-threatening`;
- `person`, `patient`, `affected person`, `dependant`, and `caregiver`;
- `may`, `can`, `often`, `is associated with`, and `causes`;
- `screening`, `assessment`, `diagnosis`, `model`, and `protocol`.

The Flo style remains warm, dry, and technically suspicious of nonsense. It
should also remain polite. The reader is not improved by being scolded while
already frightened.

Definition of done:

- one preferred phrase for each operational concept;
- automated scans for deprecated or overconfident alternatives;
- a manual pass over headings, callouts, and quick-reference cards.

### A3. Cross-reference cleanup

Work:

- verify every “go to Ch.x” instruction against the actual section title;
- add named anchors for the most common destinations;
- reduce circular references that return a reader to the same question;
- ensure the appendix index includes every generated figure and printable tool.

Definition of done:

- every route reaches an action or named service in at most three internal hops;
- no dead-end “consider seeking help” phrasing;
- a machine-readable route map can be generated from the source.

### A4. Evidence maintenance and source freshness

Extend the evidence registry with:

- `reviewed_on` per fact set;
- `operational_or_explanatory`;
- `source_type`;
- `supersedes` or `withdrawn_reason` where applicable;
- chapter sentence and figure references.

Definition of done:

- numeric prose can be traced to one registry key;
- operational guidance carries a freshness warning when overdue for review;
- the build fails when a plotted value and the registered value diverge.

### A5. Accessibility and stress readability

Improve existing content before adding a separate “accessible version”:

- alternatives to visual grounding, breath focus, body scans, and handwriting;
- explicit instructions for limited speech, hearing, vision, mobility, or German;
- shorter call scripts and a point-to-show emergency card;
- large-print and screen-reader verification of heading order and figure text.

Definition of done:

- every core calming technique has at least one non-visual and one
  non-breath-focused alternative;
- emergency actions do not depend on color, fine motor control, or fluent speech;
- the narrow edition remains readable without reducing body text below the
  verified floor.

## Track B — Extension of coverage

### B1. Environmental-hazard override gate

Add one explicit main-flowgraph branch before ordinary A–G routing:

```text
fire / smoke / carbon monoxide / gas / chemical / electrical danger?
  yes or unsure -> leave or isolate only when safe
                 -> warn others
                 -> 112 / utility / poison route as appropriate
  no            -> continue to need-based routing
```

Content packet:

- fire and smoke;
- suspected carbon monoxide;
- gas leak;
- electrical burning or live current;
- unknown chemical fumes or mixing exposure.

Source gate:

- current German fire-service, public-health, poison-centre, utility, or civil
  protection guidance;
- no concentration threshold may be used by a lay reader to clear the scene;
- no flame, switch, or improvised chemical test.

Definition of done:

- one compact visual branch and a complete text fallback;
- first action is environmental safety, not symptom classification;
- route tested in A4, A4/2, color, mono, and screen-reader order.

### B2. Dependency modifier strip

After the override, show modifiers that change logistics without inventing new
diagnoses:

$$M=\{\text{infant},\text{pregnancy/postpartum},\text{disability},
\text{medication},\text{powered device},\text{animal},\text{alone},
\text{language/access},\text{no transport}\}$$

Content packet:

- backup power and charger planning;
- essential medication continuity;
- mobility and transfer assistance;
- communication cards and door access;
- backup caregiver and animal transport.

Definition of done:

- modifiers point to adaptations inside existing chapters;
- each modifier has one preparatory action and one failure escalation;
- no sensitive personal data is required on the shared default printout.

### B3. Split the “no safe place” route

Replace one overloaded branch with four observable subroutes:

1. **No physical shelter tonight** — municipal, social, or shelter route.
2. **Violence or coercion** — safer-place and specialist-support route.
3. **Environmental exposure** — heat, cold, flood, smoke, or unsafe building.
4. **Internal crisis / nowhere feels safe** — human contact and crisis support.

Definition of done:

- each subroute provides a one-hour plan;
- each names a localizable service category;
- violence and self-harm routes retain immediate 110/112 dominance.

### B4. Evacuate versus shelter-in-place module

Add a small decision aid governed by official warnings:

- immediate local hazard can require leaving before an alert arrives;
- otherwise official instructions dominate;
- mobility, dependants, medication, animals, weather, and transport modify the
  plan;
- the module includes a compact go-bag and “cannot evacuate independently” call
  script.

Definition of done:

- no universal distance, time, or speed constants;
- full text route plus one diagram;
- apartment, vehicle, and temporary-accommodation contexts considered.

## Release acceptance

4.2.0 is ready only when:

- every new operational claim has a current authoritative source;
- every new number is registered with denominator, scope, uncertainty, and limit;
- all new flowgraph destinations have text equivalents;
- standard A4 and A4/2 color/mono builds pass renderer verification;
- no existing emergency route becomes longer or less explicit;
- changelog records both added guidance and claims deliberately not added.
