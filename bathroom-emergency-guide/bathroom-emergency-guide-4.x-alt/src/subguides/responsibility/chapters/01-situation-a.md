# Situation A — I Caused Trouble

So you've affected another sensible form of life — or something that might
become one. First: the fact that you're reading this in a bathroom suggests you
haven't fled the country, which is already a positive sign. Let's sort out what
exactly happened.

But before we sort anything:

> **Is anyone hurt, in danger, or needing medical attention right now?**
> If yes or maybe: **112**. Do not spend the useful minutes deciding whose
> fault it is.

If violence is active, leave the danger if you safely can. **110** for police;
**112** for medical rescue or when there is immediate danger to life.

Good. Now guilt wants a trial, but emergencies need a sequence. Let's give them
one.

## The five-step repair sequence

When nobody is in immediate danger, work through this:

1. **Stop** the harmful action or process.
2. **Stabilize** — prevent further harm.
3. **Tell** the affected person or responsible professional, plainly.
4. **Repair** — replace, compensate, apologize, document, or obtain care.
5. **Follow up** — check whether the repair actually worked.

This is not absolution algebra. **It is simply harder for shame to sabotage five
verbs than one enormous moral fog.**

prevent further harm.

## A1 — A life may be developing, but birth has not begun

The entity exists in potential but has not independently emerged. The word
"geschlüpft" (hatched/emerged) marks the threshold between "potential" and
"actual" life. You are at a decision point with a time window.

### Medical urgency

Call **112** for heavy bleeding, collapse, seizure, severe breathlessness,
severe or rapidly worsening pain, serious injury, or any situation that appears
life-threatening. For urgent but non-life-threatening symptoms, contact the
maternity service, gynecological practice, or **116 117** outside normal hours.

Do not use gestational-week tables as self-triage. Outcomes and urgency depend
on the individual situation and clinical assessment.

### A decision about pregnancy

This is your decision. No flowchart resolves it. What this guide *can* do is
point you toward people trained to help you think it through without judgment.

A useful appointment can cover:

- confirmation and dating;
- health and medication questions;
- available choices and legal time limits;
- financial and practical support;
- safety or coercion in the relationship;
- what the person actually wants when the room becomes quieter.

A bathroom guide can help you make the call. It should not impersonate the
appointment. Start with: your gynecologist, a family planning center (Pro
Familia in Germany, similar organizations elsewhere), or the nearest hospital.

### Medication or substance exposure

- Do not abruptly stop prescribed medication solely because of this guide.
- Contact the prescriber, maternity service, pharmacy, or a specialist source
  such as Embryotox.
- Record the product, amount, timing, and symptoms.
- For acute poisoning or serious symptoms, **112** or a poison centre (Ch.5).

### Ethical note

Whatever you decide, you are allowed to have feelings about it. Those feelings
are also worth bringing to a professional. Ch.4 (Calm Guide) if you need to
steady yourself first.

## A2 — Birth appears to be happening now

Congratulations and/or condolences — depending on context, possibly both
simultaneously. Either way, this is a medical emergency.

Call **112** and use speakerphone.

```
◆ Apocalypse status?

    ├─ Post-apocalypse? ──→ Ch.6 (Zombie Guide)
    │                      + Ch.5 (Self Ambulance Guide)
    │                      ("Self Ambulance Healing" — you're it)
    │
    └─ Pre-apocalypse? ──→ ◆ Are other humans available?
                              ├─ Yes → Call 112. Now.
                              │        Find other humans immediately.
                              │        You need support, not heroics.
                              └─ No  → Ch.5 (Self Ambulance Guide)
                                       + Ch.4 (Calm Guide)
                                       (You're doing emergency
                                        self-sufficiency. Breathe first.)
```

While waiting for help:

- keep the parent in a safe, private, warm place;
- do not pull on the baby or umbilical cord;
- support the baby if it arrives, note the time, keep parent and baby warm;
- follow the dispatcher's exact instructions;
- do not perform dramatic procedures learned from television.

If the baby is not breathing normally, tell the dispatcher immediately and
follow their resuscitation instructions. The dispatcher is now the most
qualified person in this bathroom.

**Key principle:** Minutes-old situations are time-critical. Act before you
overthink. Overthinking is a luxury available in A3 and beyond.

## A3 — A baby arrived recently: hours, days, or weeks ago

The immediate crisis has passed. What remains is the entirety of everything
else. Babies are admirably indifferent to narrative structure, so the practical
minimum is repetitive:

- feeding support and weight/health review;
- warmth and safe sleep arrangements;
- medical follow-up for parent and baby;
- sleep and relief for the caregiver;
- one person who can be called before exhaustion becomes danger.

If not already done: locate a pediatrician. The first days of a new life have
specific medical checkpoints — don't skip them.

### The ethical dimension

Here's the thing: nature's tendency to reproduce is deeply non-logical. It
doesn't reason. It just... proliferates. The fact that you're reading this in a
bathroom suggests you're doing something nature doesn't — you're *thinking*
about it. That's a fundamentally human act. Talk to someone about this. Not the
bathroom. A person.

If your mind is spiraling toward resource scarcity, environmental decay, and
whether bringing another human onto this planet is defensible — those are valid
concerns. They deserve engagement, not suppression. Ch.4 (Calm Guide) to
process before making any irreversible decisions.

### Baby blues, depression, and psychiatric emergency

A brief period of tearfulness and emotional volatility can occur after birth.
That is common and usually passes. What deserves prompt professional assessment
is persistent low mood, loss of interest, severe guilt, inability to function,
or feeling detached from the baby — especially when symptoms last beyond roughly
two weeks or are worsening.[^ppd]

**Call 112** when there is acute danger, suicidal intent, intent to harm the
baby, severe confusion, hallucinations, delusional beliefs, or a sudden loss
of contact with reality. Postpartum psychiatric emergencies are medical
conditions, not evidence that someone is "a bad parent."

For urgent support without acute danger, contact the maternity team, GP,
psychiatric service, **116 117**, or a crisis service. Arrange another adult to
stay if safety or basic care is uncertain.

WHO estimates that about **17.5% of adults — roughly one in six — experience
infertility during their lifetime**.[^infertility] That is a population estimate
over a long time frame, not the chance attached to one person, one month, or one
treatment.

A systematic review found postpartum-psychosis incidence estimates ranging from
**0.89 to 2.6 per 1,000** across five population studies.[^ppp-incidence] The
condition is uncommon and still an emergency when it appears. Rare is a frequency
word, not a permission to wait.

### Repair matters more than perfect attunement

Attachment research contains a useful relief: caregiving does not require
uninterrupted perfection. Children need sufficiently responsive care and
repeated repair after ordinary misattunements.[^attachment]

A repair can be simple:

1. return and regulate yourself;
2. acknowledge what happened in age-appropriate words;
3. reconnect through attention, comfort, or play;
4. change the practical condition that caused the rupture when possible.

Attachment categories describe research patterns. They are not labels to assign
to a child or verdicts on one difficult afternoon.

## A4 — A child or young dependant relies on you

The entity is now a walking, talking, occasionally unreasonable small human.
This is... a lot.

### Immediate safety inventory

Check:

- supervision;
- food, fluids, medication, warmth, and sleep;
- dangerous objects, substances, traffic, water, balconies, or animals;
- a trusted backup adult;
- whether the caregiver is too exhausted, intoxicated, panicked, or angry to
  remain safely in charge.

If basic care cannot be maintained today, call family support, pediatric care,
the youth welfare office, social services, or **112** when there is immediate
danger.

### "Regular development" short guide

Children are, by design, chaotic. They cry, they break things, they ask "why"
47 times in a row. This is normal. Here's a compressed timeline:

- **0-1:** Survival mode (yours and theirs). Everything is a phase.
- **1-3:** Mobility + opinions. Baby-proof everything, including your ego.
- **3-6:** Questions. So many questions. You don't need all the answers.
- **6-10:** School + social complexity. Your role shifts from caretaker to
  translator.

Development occurs in broad windows. A milestone list can help prepare a
conversation, but it cannot diagnose a child from a single observation. Contact
pediatric services when a child loses previously acquired abilities, seems
acutely ill, or when development causes persistent concern. Bring concrete
observations to the appointment. Leave "the internet says exactly 200 words"
outside with the shoes.

### State support

You may be entitled to financial and logistical support. In Germany: Kindergeld,
Elterngeld, Unterhaltsvorschuss. Look these up. They exist precisely because
this is hard. Pediatricians, family counselors, parent groups — these are not
admissions of failure. They are infrastructure. Use it.

When overwhelmed: Ch.4 (Calm Guide). You're allowed to lock the bathroom door
and breathe. That's literally why this guide is here.

### After losing your temper

If you shouted, frightened, or handled a child roughly:

1. ensure immediate safety and check for injury;
2. create distance if anger is still active;
3. ask another safe adult to take over;
4. apologize without making the child comfort you;
5. obtain professional help if this is recurring or escalation feels possible.

An apology is not "I am the worst person alive." It is: "I shouted. That was
not okay. You did not cause my behaviour. I am going to make this safer."

## A5 — The person is an adult, possibly your adult child

They're an adult. You've done your shift. Grow up, pls — and we say that with
warmth.

Responsibility changes when the other person can decide for themselves. Support
does not automatically mean control. Ask:

- Is there immediate danger or impaired capacity requiring emergency help?
- What help did the person actually request?
- What can I offer without making promises I cannot keep?
- What boundary protects both of us?
- Which professional service belongs in the gap?

A useful sentence is:

> "I care about you. I can help with **[specific action]**. I cannot decide
> this for you or carry it alone. Let us contact **[service/person]**."

"Grow up" is not a care plan. Neither is "I will secretly manage every variable
until one of us explodes." Your creation is now an autonomous agent with their
own decision-making apparatus (questionable as it may seem at times). Your
responsibility has transitioned from "keeping them alive" to "being available
if they ask."

**Practical advice.** Call them. Not to fix anything. Just to talk.

## A6 — The responsibility is ambiguous

The timeline is unclear, the situation is messy, or it doesn't fit neatly into
A1-A5. Welcome to the majority of human experience.

### I do not want this responsibility but may still have duties

Separate:

1. **today's safety duty** — the non-negotiable part;
2. **legal or contractual duty** — what's actually required;
3. **long-term willingness and capacity** — what you can sustain;
4. **who can take over or share the role** — because you shouldn't have to ask
   the bathroom.

Meet today's essential needs, then get advice. Resentment kept secret tends to
become architecture.

### I want the responsibility but cannot obtain or sustain it

This may involve infertility, adoption, money, disability, housing, care work,
or another barrier. The immediate task is not forced optimism. It is to identify
which problem is medical, legal, financial, relational, or grief-related and
contact the matching service.

Break it down:

1. **Money?** Financial barriers are real and addressable. State support,
   grants, community resources exist.
2. **Medical issues?** Fertility medicine is a vast field. Start with your GP,
   then specialize.
3. **The biology itself?** If you're here, you probably don't need the
   birds-and-bees talk — you need practical next steps.

### Nobody agrees on what happened

Write a neutral timeline:

- observable events;
- exact words or messages;
- injuries or damage;
- people present;
- actions already taken;
- what remains disputed.

Facts first. Interpretation can have a chair later.

## A7 — I harmed or may have harmed someone

This is the section where the tone shifts. Some things aren't funny, and this
is one of them.

### Accident

- Stop further danger.
- Call medical help when needed.
- Preserve facts and relevant objects or messages.
- Tell the affected person or authority honestly.
- Do not repair evidence into invisibility.

### Anger, violence, or loss of control

- Create distance and prevent access to weapons or dangerous objects if this
  can be done safely.
- Ensure children or dependants are supervised by another safe adult.
- Call **112 / 110** for immediate danger.
- Contact crisis and specialist support before returning to the same conditions.

### Self-defence, coercion, or unclear legal exposure

Get safe, obtain medical care, preserve evidence, and seek legal advice. Do not
rely on a compressed paragraph about German self-defence law to decide what to
say or do in a specific case. Ch.7 explains how to find legal support without
this guide becoming a confident but unlicensed barrister.

```
◆ Why did this happen?

    ├─ Self-defence?
    │     ├─ Legal dimension → Consult local self-defence laws.
    │     │   In Germany: §32 StGB (Notwehr) applies.
    │     │   Contact a lawyer (Ch.7).
    │     │   + Ch.4 — you've been through something traumatic.
    │     └─ Ethical dimension → Even justified harm carries weight.
    │         Talk to someone trained for this (Ch.7).
    │
    ├─ Accident?
    │     ├─ Imminent danger? → Act now:
    │     │     Call 112. Ch.5 (Self Ambulance Guide).
    │     │     Find other humans immediately.
    │     └─ Aftermath → You need both practical and emotional
    │         support. Start with Ch.4, then Ch.7.
    │
    └─ For fun / profit?
          → §
          This guide has nothing for you.
          Leave the bathroom. Reconsider your choices.
          Come back when you're ready to be human.
```

### Thoughts of harming yourself or another person

Do not stay alone with the means to act. Create distance, involve another
adult, and call **112** for acute danger. When there is no immediate danger but
the thoughts are frightening or recurring, contact **116 123**, **116 117**, a
local crisis service, or a psychiatric emergency department.

## A8 — Ongoing responsibility for a person, animal, or system

Ongoing responsibility is less dramatic than A7 but can be just as heavy. The
weight doesn't arrive all at once — it accumulates. Caregiver strain is not a
character defect. It is a load problem. Load problems need relief, rotation, and
services — not more private heroism.[^caregiver]

### Responsibility dashboard

| Domain | Question | Action when failing |
|---|---|---|
| Safety | Can everyone remain safe today? | emergency or backup care |
| Essentials | Food, medication, hygiene, shelter available? | arrange service or supply |
| Capacity | Can I continue without becoming unsafe? | respite, rotation, counselling |
| Knowledge | Do I understand the care task? | clinician, vet, training, written plan |
| Authority | Am I actually permitted to decide this? | clarify consent, custody, contract |
| Continuity | What happens if I become ill tonight? | backup person and contact sheet |
| Repair | What recurring failure needs a system change? | simplify, delegate, document |

### Minimum viable care plan

Write:

- daily essentials;
- medications and allergies;
- warning signs;
- emergency contacts;
- backup caregiver;
- where documents and supplies live;
- one source of respite.

If the plan exists only in one exhausted person's head, it is not yet a plan.
It is a single point of failure wearing slippers.

### Animals

For acute breathing difficulty, collapse, major trauma, poisoning, uncontrolled
bleeding, seizures, or severe pain, contact a veterinary emergency service.
Protect yourself from frightened bites and scratches. Do not give human
medication unless a veterinarian specifically instructs you.

### You matter too

This is not selfish. An empty vessel can't pour. Take care of yourself so you
can take care of them. Ch.4 (Calm Guide) for daily maintenance; Ch.7 for
structural support.

## A9 — A silicon-based life form escaped

Fine. The guide accepts the science-fiction branch in full.

1. Disconnect or isolate the affected system if authorized and safe.
2. Stop automated actions, external access, or credential use where possible.
3. Do not wipe it, "test one more thing," or publish credentials.
4. Preserve logs, prompts, model/version information, and timestamps.
5. Notify the system owner or incident-response contact.
6. Record affected accounts, data, devices, and observed behaviour.
7. For a serious cyber incident, follow BSI and organizational reporting routes.

Contain first. Post-mortem later. The server does not need your guilt, only its
logs.

## The accountability theorem

Self-punishment and repair are not the same variable.

**Remorse does not equal repair.**

Useful accountability increases truth, safety, restitution, and changed
behaviour. If an action only increases your suffering while changing nothing
for the affected party, it may be punishment — but it is not yet repair.

## When guilt itself becomes the emergency

Guilt can motivate repair. Shame can also become immobilizing, obsessive, or
suicidal. If you are stuck in repetitive self-punishment, unable to function, or
at risk of harming yourself, use Ch.4 and Ch.7. Acute danger means **112**.

You are still responsible for what happened. You are also still a person who
needs enough stability to do the repair.
